
1. Let x and y be differentiable functions of t, and let $s = \sqrt{5x^2+3y^2}$ be a function of $x$ and $y$. Answer the following questions.
	a. How is $ds \over dt$ related to $dx \over dt$ if $y$ is constant?
	${ds \over dt} =$
	$$\begin{aligned}
	{ds \over dt} &= {1\over2}(5x^2+3y^2)^{-1/2} \cdot {d\over dt}(5x^2+3y^2) \\\\
	&\to {d\over dt}(5x^2+3y^2)=10x{dx\over dt}+0 \\\\
	{ds \over dt} &= {1\over2}(5x^2+3y^2)^{-1/2} \cdot (10x{dx\over dt}) \\\\
	{ds\over dt} &= (5x^2+3y^2)^{-1/2} \cdot 5x{dx \over dt} \\\\
	&={5x\over\sqrt{5x^2+3y^2}} \cdot {dx \over dt}
	\end{aligned}$$
	b. How is $ds \over dt$ related to $dx \over dt$ and $dy \over dt$ if neither x nor y is constant?
	${ds \over dt}=$
	$$\begin{aligned}
	{ds \over dt} &= {1\over2}(5x^2+3y^2)^{-1/2} \cdot {d\over dt}(5x^2+3y^2) \\\\
	&\to {d\over dt}(5x^2+3y^2) = 5{d\over dt}x^2+3{d\over dt}y^2\\\\
	&= 10x \cdot {dx\over dt}+6y\cdot {dy\over dt} \\\\
	{ds \over dt} &= {1\over2}\left(5x^2+3y^2\right)^{-1/2} \cdot \left(10x \cdot {dx\over dt}+6y\cdot {dy\over dt}\right) \\\\
	&= \left(5x^2+3y^2\right)^{-1/2} \cdot \left(5x \cdot {dx\over dt}+3y\cdot {dy\over dt}\right) \\\\
	&= \left(5x^2+3y^2\right)^{-1/2}\cdot(5x\cdot{dx \over dt})+\left(5x^2+3y^2\right)^{-1/2}\cdot(3y\cdot{dy \over dt}) \\\\
	&= \frac{3y}{\sqrt{5x^2+3y^2}} \cdot {dy \over dt} + \frac{5x}{\sqrt{5x^2+3y^2}} {dx \over dt} \\\\
	\end{aligned}$$
	c. How is ${dx\over dt}$ related to ${dy \over dt}$ if $s$ is constant?
	${dx \over dt} =$
	$$\begin{aligned}
	s &= (5x^2+3y^2)^{1/2} \\\\
	{dx\over dt} &= {d\over dt}\left((5x^2+3y^2)^{1/2}\right) \\\\
	&= {1\over2}(5x^2+3y^2)^{-1/2} \cdot \left(5{d\over dt}(x^2)+3{d\over dt}(y^2)\right) \\\\
	&= {1\over2}(5x^2+3y^2)^{-1/2} \cdot \left(10x{dx\over dt}+6y{dy\over dt}\right) \\\\
	{ds\over dt} &= 0 \quad \text{(because s is constant)} \\\\
	0 &= {1\over2}(5x^2+3y^2)^{-1/2} \cdot \left(10x{dx\over dt}+6y{dy\over dt}\right) \\\\
	0 &= 10x{dx\over dt}+6y{dy\over dt} \\\\
	10x{dx\over dt} &= -6y{dy\over dt} \\\\
	{dx \over dt} &= \boxed{-\frac{3y}{5x} \cdot \frac{dy}{dt}}
	\end{aligned}$$

3.  When a circular plate of metal is heated is an oven, its radius increases at a rate of 0.03 cm/min. At what rate is the plate's area increasing when the radius 41 cm? 
	Write an equation relating the area of the circular plate, A, and the radius, r
	${dr\over dt} = 0.03 \frac{\text{cm}}{\text{min}}$
	$r=41\text{cm}$
	$A=\pi r^2$
	$$\begin{aligned}
	{dA\over dt} &= \pi{d\over dt}(r^2) \\\\
	&= 2\pi r \cdot {dr\over dt} \\\\
	&= 2\pi(41)(0.03) \\\\
	{dA\over dt} &= 2.46\pi \frac{\text{cm}^2}{\text{min}}
	\end{aligned}$$

4. A child flies a kite at a height of 80 ft, the wind carrying the kite horizontally away from the child at a rate of 34 ft/sec. How fast must the child let out string when the kite is 170 ft away from the child?
The child must let out the string at a rate of __ ft/sec when the kite is 170 ft away from the child.
$y =$ height of the kite
$x =$ horizontal position of the kite
$s =$ length of string 
$y = 80ft$ (constant, which means ${dy\over dx} = 0$)
${dx\over dt}=34 \frac{\text{ft}}{\text{sec}}$
$x^2+y^2=s^2 = 170$ 
$x^2=-80^2+170^2 \to x = 150$
$$\begin{aligned}
{d\over dt}s^2 &= {d\over dt}(x^2)+{d\over dt}(y^2) \\\\
2s{ds\over dt} &= 2x{dx\over dt} +2y{dy\over dt} \\\\
{ds\over dt} &= {x(34 \frac{\text{ft}}{\text{sec}})\over s} +0 \\\\
&= \frac{150\cancel{\text{ ft}}(34 \frac{\text{ft}}{\text{sec}})}{170\cancel{\text{ ft}}} \\\\
&= {5100\over 170}\frac{\text{ft}}{\text{sec}} \\\\
&= \boxed{ 30 \frac{\text{ft}}{\text{sec}}}
\end{aligned}$$


5. A spherical balloon is inflating with helium at a rate of $48\pi \text{ ft}^3 / \text{min}$. How fast is the balloon's radius increasing as the instant the radius is 2 ft? How fast is the surface area increasing?
	The balloon's radius is increasing at a rate of __ ft/min at the instant the radius is 2 ft.
	Volume growth: ${dV \over dt} = 48\pi \text{ ft}^3/\text{min}$
	Radius $r = 2$ ft
	Volume $V = {4\over3}\pi r^3$
	Radius growth at $2$ ft $= 3$ ft/min
	Area $A = 4\pi r^2$
	$$\begin{aligned}
	&\text{=== Volume ===}\\\\
	{d \over dt}V &= {4\pi \over3}\cdot{d \over dt}( r^3)\\\\
	{dV \over dt} &=  {4\pi \over3}3r^2{dr \over dt} \\\\
	{dV \over dt} &=4\pi r^2 {dr \over dt} \\\\
	48\pi \frac{\text{ ft}^3}{\text{min}} &= 16 \text{ ft}^2\pi{dr \over dt} \\\\
	{dr\over dt} &= \frac{48\pi \text{ ft}^3}{\text{min}(16 \text{ ft}^2\pi)} \\\\
	{dr\over dt} &= \boxed{ 3\frac{\text{ft}}{\text{min}} }
	
	 \\\\ &\text{=== Area ===} \\\\
	{d \over dt}A &= 4\pi{d \over dt}( r^2) \\\\
	{dA \over dt} &= 8\pi r {dr \over dt} \\\\
	{dA \over dt} &= 8 \pi (2 \text{ft})(\frac{3\text{ ft}}{\text{min}}) \\\\
	&= \boxed{ 48 \pi\frac{\text{ft}^2}{\text{min}}}
	\end{aligned}$$
5. A dinghy is pulled toward a dock by a rope from the bow through a ring on the dock 6 ft above the bow. The rope is hauled in at a rate of 2 ft/sec. Complete parts (a) and (b).
	a. At what rate is the distance between the dinghy and the dock changing when 10 ft of rope are out? 
	__ ft /sec
	Horizontal distance between dock to dinghy bow $= x$
	Vertical distance from bow to ring $= y = 6$ ft (constant means ${dy \over dt} = 0$ )
	Angle from boat bow to ring: $\theta$
	Rope length (hypotenuse h) from boat  bow to ring $= 10$ ft
	Rope length (hypotenuse h) from boat bow to ring change $= {dh\over dt} = -2$ ft/sec
	$x^2+y^2=h^2$
	$x^2 = -6^2 +10^2 \to 8 = x$
	$\sin(\theta) = {y\over h} = {6\over h}$
	$\cos(\theta)= {4\over5}$
	$$\begin{aligned}
	&\text{Horizontal Distance} \\\\
	{d \over dt}h^2 &= {d\over dt}x^2+{d\over dt}y^2 \\\\
	2h{dh \over dt} &=2x{dx \over dt} +2y {dy \over dt} \\\\
	2h{dh \over dt} &=2x{dx \over dt} + 0 \\\\
	2(10 \text{ ft})(-\frac{2\text{ ft}}{\text{sec}}) &= 2(8 \text{ ft}){dx \over dt} \\\\
	{dx \over dt} &=-\frac{40 \text{ ft}}{\text{sec}(16)} \\\\
	{dx \over dt} &=\boxed{-2.5 {\text{ft}\over\text{sec}}} \\\\
	&\text{=== Angle ===} \\\\
	{d\over dt}\sin(\theta) &= 6{d\over dt}(h^{-1}) \\\\
	\cos(\theta){d\theta \over dt} &= -{6 \over h^2}{dh \over dt} \\\\
	{4\over5}{d\theta \over dt} &= -\frac{6}{100}(-2) \\\\
	{d\theta \over dt} &= {12\over100}\cdot{5\over4} \\\\
	{d\theta \over dt} &= {3\over20}rad/sec
	\end{aligned}$$
6. The original 24 m edge length x of a cube decreases at the rate of 4 m/min
	Area $A = 6x^2$
	Volume $V = x^3$
	Side length (original) $= 24$m, (now) $= 1$m
	Change in side length ${dx\over dt} = -4$m/min
	
	a. When $x = 1$m, at what rate does the cube's surface area change?
	$$\begin{aligned}
	{d \over dt}A &= 6{d \over dt}x^2 \\\\
	{dA \over dt} &= 12x{dx \over dt} \\\\
	{dA \over dt} &= -48m /min
	\end{aligned}$$
	b. When $x= 1$m, at what rate does the cube's volume change?
	$$\begin{aligned}
	{dV \over dt} &= {d\over dt}x^3 \\\\
	{dV \over dt} &= 3x^2 {dx\over dt} \\\\
	{dV \over dt} &= 4m^2({-4m/min}) \\\\
	&= -{16 m^3/min}
	\end{aligned}$$
