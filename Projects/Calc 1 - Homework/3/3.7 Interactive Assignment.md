1. Use implicit differentiation to find $dy\over dx$
$(5xy+4)^2=10y$
$$\begin{aligned}
{dy\over dx} &= {d\over dx}(5xy+4)^2 = {d\over dx}10y \\\\
&=(2)(5xy+4){d\over dx}(5xy+4)=10 {dy\over dx} \\\\
&=(2)(5xy+4)(5)(x{dy\over dx}+y)=10{dy\over dx} \\\\
&= 10(5xy+4)(x{dy\over dx}+y)=10{dy\over dx} \\\\
&=10(5x^2yy'+5xy^2+4xy'+4y)=10y' \\
&=5x^2yy'+5xy^2+4xy'+4y=y' \\
&= 5xy^2+4y=y'-5x^2yy'-4xy' \\
&= 5xy^2+4y=y'(1-5x^2y-4x) \\\\
{dy \over dx}&= \frac{5xy^2+4y}{1-5x^2y-4x}
\end{aligned}$$
2. Find $dy \over dx$ by implicit differentiation for the following equation.
$e^{x^4y}=8x+6y+2$
$$\begin{aligned}
{dy\over dx} &= {d\over dx}(e^{x^4y})={d\over dx}(8x+6y+2) \\
&= e^{x^4y}{d\over dx}(x^4y)=8+6{dy\over dx} \\
&= (e^{x^4y})({dy\over dx}x^4+4x^3y)=6{dy\over dx}+8 \\
&= y'x^4e^{x^4y}+4x^3ye^{x^4y}=6y'+8 \\
&= 4x^3ye^{x^4y}-8 = 6y'-y'x^4e^{x^4y} \\
&= 4x^3ye^{x^4y}-8 = y'(6-x^4e^{x^4y})\\\\
{dy\over dx} &= \frac{4x^3ye^{x^4y}-8}{6-x^4e^{x^4y}} = y'
\end{aligned}$$
3. Find $dr\over d\theta$ for $\theta ^{5/8} -r^{5/8} = 4$
$$\begin{aligned}
{dr\over d\theta}&={d\over d\theta}(\theta^{5/8})-{d\over d\theta}(r^{5/8})= {d\over d\theta}4 \\\\
&\to {5\over8}\theta^{-3/8}-{5\over8}r^{-3/8}({dr\over d\theta})=0 \\\\
&\to {5\over8}\theta^{-3/8} ={5\over8}r^{-3/8}({dr\over d\theta}) \\\\
&\to \frac{{5\over8}\theta^{-3/8}}{{5\over8}r^{-3/8}}={dr\over d\theta} \\\\
&\to \frac{\theta^{-3/8}}{r^{-3/8}}={dr\over d\theta} \\\\
&\to \frac{\theta^{-3/8}}{r^{-3/8}}={dr\over d\theta} \\\\
&\to \left(\frac{r}{\theta}\right)^{3/8}= {dr\over d\theta}
\end{aligned}$$

4. Find the slope of the curve at the given point.
$7y^7+3x^7=4y+6x$ at $(1,1)$
$$\begin{aligned} 
y'(1) &= 7{d\over dx}y^7+3{d\over dx}x^7= 4{dy\over dx}+6{d\over dx}x \\\\
 &= {dy\over dx}49y^6+21x^6=4{dy\over dx}+6 \\\\
 &= {dy\over dx}49(1)^6+21(1)^6=4{dy\over dx}+6 \\\\
 &= 45 {dy\over dx} +21=6 \\
 &= 45 {dy\over dx} = -15 \\
 &= {dy\over dx} = -{15\over45}  \\\\
{dy\over dx} &=-{1\over3}
\end{aligned}$$

5. Verify that the given point is on the curve and find lines that are (a) tangent and (b) normal to the curve at the given point.
$x^2 y^2 = 100$, $(-5,2)$
The point is on the curve because when ___ is substituted for x and ___ is substituted for y, the resulting statement is ___ = 100 which is a ___ statement.

The point is on the curve because when -5 is substituted for x and 2 is substituted for y, the resulting statement is 100 = 100 which is a true statement.

$$\begin{aligned}
{dy \over dx} &= {d\over dx}(x^2y^2) = {d\over dx}100 \\
&\to  {d\over dx}(y^2)x^2+{d\over dx}(x^2)y^2 = 0 \\
&\to 2yy'x^2+2xy^2 = 0 \\
&\to 2xy^2 = -2yy'x^2\\
&\to y=-y'x \\
&\to y=y'(-x) \\
&\to -\frac{y}{x}=y'
\end{aligned}$$
